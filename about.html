Story & Philosophy

Lead Developer

Alex

Ukrainian developer, born in 2011. I started this project at 12 years old, completely alone. This page is me talking to you directly - no PR language, no marketing. Just the story of how and why this all exists.

Chapter 1

A Cracked Xbox and a Kid Who Fell in Love

Let me take you back to around 2016–2017. My parents bought a cracked Xbox 360. Nothing fancy - it wasn't even a legitimate console. But on that thing, I played Minecraft for the very first time. And something clicked in me that has never really stopped clicking since. I was completely, utterly in love with that game. I still am.

Years passed. I kept playing Minecraft on and off, across different devices and versions. And then, in 2023, I stumbled across something that hit me the same way Minecraft did back in 2016 - HBM's Nuclear Tech Mod.

I was blown away. I couldn't believe a mod like this existed. The machines, the processing chains, the sheer depth of it all. I was so amazed that I didn't even think twice - I started building a modpack around it almost immediately. That first version wasn't anything special. Just about 25 mods thrown together with no real structure or direction. But I didn't care. I was having fun, and I was dragging my friends in to experience NTM with me.

🎂
September 11, 2023 - The Official Birthday

That's the date I created NTM RP - the very first version of what would eventually become NTNH. Technically, that makes it the birthday of the entire project. I even made an old trailer in Russian for it. It was pretty bad, honestly. Maybe I'll show it someday, for the laughs.

The first proper concept was called NTM RP. The idea: players would roleplay countries - real cities, professions, diplomacy, wars - all built on NTM's foundation. On paper it sounded genuinely exciting. In practice... it didn't hold together. So after about a month, I renamed it to NuclearCraft Project.

And that's where it got real. NuclearCraft Project was the first time I started genuinely investing myself into this. Not just throwing mods together - but thinking about progression, about what kind of experience I wanted players to have, about what made NTM special and how to build around that. I was still learning. Still making plenty of mistakes. But the soul of the project was there, from that point on.

Chapter 2

Six Generations

The project didn't become NTNH overnight. It went through six distinct generations - each one a full rethink, a new direction, a different Minecraft version. Here's the full lineage, warts and all.

September 11, 2023 · Origin
NTM RP ★ Birthday
Minecraft 1.12.2 using NTM: Extended Edition. A roleplay modpack - nations, cities, wars, professions. The concept had heart. The execution didn't quite work. Lasted about a month before I knew I had to rethink everything.
Generation 1
NuclearCraft Project 1.12.2
The rename that changed everything. First real attempt at building something with intention. NTM: Extended Edition, Minecraft 1.12.2. First real progression ideas. First time the project felt like it had a direction - and like it was genuinely mine.
Generation 2
NuclearCraft Project 1.12.2
Full content overhaul. Better quests, tighter progression, more polish. Same 1.12.2 foundation with NTM: Extended Edition - but noticeably more deliberate.
Generation 3
NuclearCraft Project 1.7.10
A big jump to Minecraft 1.7.10 and the original HBM's NTM. Completely different ecosystem - older, more classic modded Minecraft. It taught me a lot about what the original mod was capable of on its own terms.
Generation 4
NuclearCraft Project 1.12.2 + 1.7.10
The ambitious one. Dual-version development - 1.12.2 running NTM: Space instead of regular NTM, and 1.7.10 running alongside it in parallel. I was trying to do too much at once. Chaotic in hindsight. But the experience was invaluable.
Generation 5
NuclearCraft Project 1.12.2
Back to a single version. Real momentum - but also where things fell apart personally. The generation that led directly to burnout, and eventually to everything changing. More on that next.
The Rebirth
Nuclear Tech: New Horizons Current
Not a new generation - a completely new identity. New name, new vision, new purpose. Built on 1.7.10 with original HBM's NTM. Inspired by what GregTech: New Horizons did for GregTech - and determined to do the same for Nuclear Tech.
A note on versions: Minecraft 1.12.2 builds used NTM: Extended Edition by Alcater. Minecraft 1.7.10 builds used the original HBM's Nuclear Tech Mod. Between every generation there were full rewrites and wipes. I worked through all of it alone.

Chapter 3

Burnout. Silence. Coming Back Differently.

I want to be honest about this part, because I think it matters.

By the end of the fifth generation, I had run out of steam. Not because I stopped caring about NTM or Minecraft - I never stopped caring. But I'd been working on this completely alone for a long time. I had no clear idea where to take the modpack next. And on top of that, a small number of people who seemed to exist specifically to throw meaningless, baseless criticism at everything I made. Not useful feedback - just noise. And after a while, that kind of noise adds up.

I disappeared. Went quiet for about a month. Just... stopped.

Honest confession: I wasn't a great developer back then. And I wasn't a great community admin either. I was figuring things out in public, making mistakes in public, and not always handling it well. That's the truth, and I'd rather say it than pretend otherwise.

And then I came across Nuclear Tech: Integrated - a modpack by Skippy. I want to say this clearly: Skippy, if you're reading this - thank you. That modpack reminded me what was possible when you approach NTM with real creative focus and intention. It reminded me why I started all of this. It pulled me back in.

But I didn't come back to keep doing what I'd been doing. I came back with a completely different question in my head:

"What would happen if someone made GTNH, but with Nuclear Tech Mod at the core instead of GregTech?"

- Me, somewhere in late 2023

I'd heard a lot about GregTech: New Horizons by that point. I knew what made it remarkable - a modpack built entirely around the identity of one specific mod, taken to its absolute limit, with guided progression and real mechanical depth. And I thought: NTM deserves exactly that. NTM has always deserved that.

So that's where Nuclear Tech: New Horizons came from. Not a new generation of NuclearCraft - something genuinely new. A project with a clear identity and a clear purpose, with a name that says exactly what it is.

Chapter 4

What I Actually Believe

These aren't things I wrote because they sound good. They're things I genuinely believe, based on where I came from and what I've seen.

01

NTNH will always be free. No exceptions.

I grew up in a low-income family. I know exactly what it's like to want something and not be able to afford it - especially online, when your parents are skeptical of internet payments in general. There are kids right now in exactly that position.

NTNH is built entirely on free, open-source mods that other people made and gave to the community for nothing. It would be genuinely wrong to take that work and charge money for it. I'm not willing to do that. Not now, not ever.

Want to donate? We have a Ko-fi. I'll be grateful for it. But it will never be required. No paywalls. No locked content. No advantages for supporters. This is a tip jar - nothing more.

02

HBM's Nuclear Tech Mod is criminally underrated - and that needs to change.

This mod has been in active development for over 10 years. It has deep, layered factory gameplay. It has lore. It has a real backstory. It has easter eggs and hidden references that most players never even stumble across.

And almost nobody outside of small modded Minecraft communities knows it exists. Most people who've heard of it at all think it's just "a mod that lets you blow things up with nukes." That description misses everything that makes it great.

This is not a bomb mod. It's a full industrial simulator with a storyline running underneath it - if you know where to look. The engineering chains alone are more complex and rewarding than most mainstream mods even attempt to be.

"Did you know NTM has lore? A full backstory, hidden in the world? Did you know how many easter eggs and references are buried in this mod that almost nobody ever finds?"

- Something I want every new player to eventually discover on their own
03

Our goal is to put NTM on the map.

Every player who discovers HBM's Nuclear Tech Mod through NTNH is a real win. Not just for this modpack - but for the mod itself, and for the people who have spent over a decade building it. They deserve that recognition.

If NTNH can do for NTM what GTNH did for GregTech - build something so compelling around it that people simply can't ignore the underlying mod anymore - that would mean everything to me.

Chapter 5

And Now, Here We Are

A project I started at 12 years old, alone, with 25 mods and no real plan - has turned into this. A proper website. A large, active Discord. A multiplayer server. Translations in over 20 languages. Our own AI bot. A real development team. 300+ quests with real, guided progression.

And recently - something that means a lot to me - new people have started joining the project. Not just players, but contributors and developers who share the same vision. Who understand what this is supposed to be. Who believe in the same things I believe in.

We're like a family running a family business - except we don't charge anyone for anything. I think there's something really special about that. Something worth protecting.

What exists as of today

Is this what happiness looks like? Yeah. I think it is. I started this because I fell in love with a game on a cracked Xbox, and then fell in love again with a mod that almost nobody knew about. And I just kept going - through the bad versions, through the burnout, through building everything completely alone for months.

I'm happy I kept going. And I'm not done.

A shoutout to Skippy - the person whose work pulled me back when I was ready to stop. Go check out Nuclear Tech: Integrated.
